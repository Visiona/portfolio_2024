UX Case Study

Accenture Onboarding Hub

A centralised onboarding experience that helped new joiners find key information, navigate essential resources and feel confident during their first weeks.

UX Research UX / UI Design Information Architecture SharePoint
Role UX Research, UX Design, UI Design
Team Project Manager, Product Owner, Web Developer
Platform SharePoint internal site
Focus Onboarding, IA, self-service resources
{% include project-proof.html project="onboarding" %}

Context

New joiners were losing time across scattered resources.

The client had several sites intended for new joiners, but information was scattered across different platforms and emails. During onboarding, new hires received multiple links to different resources, which created confusion and lowered engagement.

This fragmented approach led to a high volume of repeated inquiries to the Careers team and feedback from new hires that essential information was difficult to find.

Goals

Create one clearer place for onboarding information.

  • Centralise scattered resources into a single user-friendly platform.
  • Improve information accessibility through a clearer information architecture.
  • Reduce repetitive inquiries to the Careers team using self-service resources.
  • Improve the onboarding experience through feedback, content and navigation changes.

Research

The research showed that the issue was not content volume, but findability.

To understand low engagement and high inquiry rates, I used interviews, usability testing, surveys and analytics review. The goal was to understand where new joiners struggled and what information they expected to find first.

01

Fragmented sources

New joiners received many links and had to move between platforms to build a complete picture.

02

Low confidence

Users were unsure whether they had found the most current or relevant onboarding material.

03

Repeated questions

HR and Careers teams received recurring questions that could be reduced through clearer self-service content.

04

Navigation friction

Important links, event materials and basic information were not surfaced at the right moment.

User interviews

Listening to new hires and the Careers team.

I conducted one-on-one interviews with recent new hires and members of the Careers team. The conversations focused on how users accessed onboarding resources and what they found difficult to locate.

  • How did you access onboarding resources?
  • Which information was difficult to locate?
  • How did you feel about the induction materials?

Survey & analytics

Survey responses confirmed recurring usability and engagement issues.

Existing onboarding workshop surveys helped reveal repeated patterns around navigation, communication and engagement with the portal.

Empathy map & persona

Turning research observations into a clearer user model.

I used empathy mapping and a persona to summarise the needs, behaviours and concerns of new joiners navigating the onboarding process.

Journey map

Mapping the onboarding journey revealed where support was needed most.

I mapped the onboarding journey to understand how new joiners moved through the experience, where they needed basic information and where deeper material should be placed.

  • Prioritise basic information early in the information architecture.
  • Group advanced materials deeper within the navigation.
  • Use onboarding modules, quizzes and virtual events to improve engagement.

Information architecture

The final structure had to turn many resources into a navigable system.

After reviewing the available HR materials, I organised the content using information architecture principles. The volume of information had to be split into multiple areas, with the homepage acting as a high-level gateway to detailed subpages.

Low-fidelity prototype & usability testing

Testing helped identify the practical links and content users expected upfront.

Based on interviews, sitemap work and available materials, I created a low-fidelity prototype and invited four employees from different locations and levels to navigate it and share feedback.

  • Users wanted crucial links, such as Time & Expense, to be easier to access.
  • Event-related materials needed to appear alongside the relevant agenda items.
  • Users requested additional resource topics they commonly searched for.

Results

Improved onboarding support and a repeatable internal site model.

The client confirmed that the portal facilitated onboarding, saved time for new hires and simplified navigation through company resources. The original UK site also influenced the Irish team, who replicated the structure for their own materials.

>€12k Additional saving by replicating the UK site into the Irish branch
>83% Increase in positive feedback
>53% Drop in onboarding communication with HR
>660% Increase in clicks on crucial resources

High-fidelity prototypes & launch

After testing, we refined, built and launched the final SharePoint site.

We conducted an additional round of testing on high-fidelity prototypes, discussed findings with stakeholders and implemented changes before launch.

Reflection

The strongest design decision was structure.

This project showed that onboarding problems are often not solved by adding more content. They are solved by ordering information better, reducing uncertainty and helping users know where to go next.
